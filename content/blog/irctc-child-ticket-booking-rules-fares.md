Under Indian Railways rules, **children under 5 years travel completely free** without a separate berth, though you should add them as an infant during booking. The **train half ticket age is 5 to 11 years** (5 years completed, under 12): you pay **50% of the adult base fare** without a berth (NOSB), or **100% full adult fare** if you want a dedicated berth. Passengers aged **12 years and above always pay full adult fare**, and age is strictly calculated on the **date of journey**, not the booking date.

## What Is the Train Half Ticket Age Limit in Indian Railways?

**The train half ticket age limit in Indian Railways is 5 to 11 years (meaning a child who has completed 5 years but is under 12 on the journey date). Children under 5 travel free without a seat, while anyone aged 12 years or older must buy a full adult ticket.**

Understanding the three statutory age brackets prevents overpaying for toddlers or facing fines from a Travelling Ticket Examiner (TTE) for older children. Since the 2016 Railway Board fare revision, half-fare tickets no longer include an independent sleeping berth, so parents must explicitly choose between half fare without a berth or full fare with a berth.

| Child Age (on Journey Date)    | Ticket Category            | Berth / Seat Provided?      | Fare Charged on IRCTC               |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| **0 to 4 Years (Under 5)**     | Infant (Free)              | No (Shares adult berth)     | **₹0 (Free)**                       |
| **0 to 4 Years (Optional)**    | Full Child Berth           | Yes (Dedicated berth/seat)  | **100% Adult Fare**                 |
| **5 to 11 Years (No Berth)**   | Child Half Ticket (`NOSB`) | No (Shares adult berth)     | **50% Base Fare + Full Taxes/Fees** |
| **5 to 11 Years (With Berth)** | Child Full Berth           | Yes (Dedicated berth/seat)  | **100% Adult Fare**                 |
| **12 Years and Above**         | Adult Passenger            | Yes (Compulsory berth/seat) | **100% Adult Fare**                 |

Notice that "half ticket" means **50% of the basic adult fare** when you opt out of a separate berth. Fixed ancillary fees, such as reservation charges, superfast surcharges, and applicable GST in AC classes, can still apply per ticket rules, so the final total is roughly half rather than an exact 50% split of the grand total.

## Do Children Under 5 Years Need a Train Ticket in India?

**Children under 5 years of age do not need a paid train ticket in India if they share a berth with an accompanying adult. However, you should still add their name, age, and gender via the "Add Infant without Berth" option on IRCTC so they appear on the reservation chart.**

Adding an under-5 infant costs zero rupees, and it keeps your family travel record accurate on the TTE's Handheld Terminal (HHT). If you leave an infant off the booking, you still won't be fined for fare evasion, but registering them ensures they are included in the official passenger manifest for safety and railway emergency records.

1. **Free Travel for 0 to 4 Years:** A 4-year-old child rides completely free in Sleeper (`SL`), AC 3-Tier (`3A`), AC 3-Economy (`3E`), Second AC (`2A`), First AC (`1A`), and Chair Car (`CC`) when sharing with a parent.
2. **When to Pay Full Fare for a Toddler:** On overnight 14-to-24-hour journeys, sharing a 72-cm-wide Sleeper or 3A berth with an active 3- or 4-year-old gets cramped quickly. If you want a separate berth for your toddler, enter them under the regular passenger list and select the berth option; IRCTC will bill 100% adult fare.
3. **Unreserved & Metro/Suburban Travel:** On unreserved General (`GS`) tickets booked via station counters or the RailOne/UTS app, children under 5 also travel free without a ticket.

## Does a Half Ticket (Ages 5 to 11) Include a Separate Seat or Berth?

**No, a half-fare train ticket for children aged 5 to 11 does not include a separate berth or seat. Indian Railways prints the status as NOSB (No Seat/Berth) on the PNR, meaning your child must share your assigned berth throughout the journey.**

This rule catches many families off guard when they board the train expecting a half-price berth. Before 2016, half tickets came with a full berth, which led to berth shortages across busy routes. Today, booking a separate berth for a 5-to-11-year-old requires paying the full adult fare.

Deciding between half fare (`NOSB`) and full fare depends on journey duration and coach class:

- **Short Daytime Journeys (Under 6 Hours):** Booking a half ticket without a berth works well in Sleeper or 3A when your child can sit beside you during daytime hours.
- **Overnight Trips (8+ Hours):** Paying 100% fare for a dedicated berth is worth it for children aged 7 to 11. Trying to share one narrow side berth or middle berth overnight leaves both parent and child exhausted.
- **Chair Car Classes (`CC`, `EC`, Vande Bharat, Shatabdi):** Seated Intercity trains assign individual bucket seats per passenger, so the berthless half-fare `NOSB` option is generally not available in Chair Car (`CC`) or Executive Class (`EC`). Every child requiring a seat aged 5 to 11 pays full fare on seated express services.

> **Pro-Tip for Families:** If you book two adult Lower Berths and add one child aged 5–11 on a half ticket (`NOSB`), the child can comfortably share the wide Lower Berth space during the day without climbing ladders. Use the "Book only if at least 1 lower berth is allotted" preference on IRCTC during checkout.

## Is Child Age Calculated on the Booking Date or Journey Date?

**Indian Railways calculates a child's age strictly on the date of the train journey, not on the date you book the ticket. If your child turns 5 or 12 between the 60-day advance booking date and departure day, you must enter the age they will be on boarding day.**

Because India's Advance Reservation Period (ARP) allows bookings up to **60 days in advance**, birthdays often fall in the gap between purchasing a ticket and stepping onto the platform. Entering the booking-day age by mistake causes an age mismatch during onboard verification.

Consider these two common birthday scenarios:

- **Turning 5 Before Travel:** Your child is 4 years and 11 months old when you book two months ahead, but turns 5 two days before departure. You cannot travel free as an infant; you must book a 5-year-old child ticket (half fare `NOSB` or full fare with berth).
- **Turning 12 Before Travel:** Your child is 11 when you book, but turns 12 before the train departs. They are legally an adult passenger on travel day and require a **100% full adult ticket** with a seat. If you booked a half-fare `NOSB` ticket, the TTE can treat it as underpaid travel and charge the fare difference plus penalty.

## How Much Does a Child Ticket Cost in Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Vande Bharat, and Garib Rath?

**Child ticket pricing varies by train category: standard Mail/Express and Garib Rath trains allow 50% berthless half fares for ages 5–11, while Vande Bharat and Shatabdi Chair Cars require full fares for individual seats. On Rajdhani and Vande Bharat, full-berth child tickets also include default catering charges unless you opt out.**

Checking train-specific rules before paying prevents unexpected surcharges at checkout, especially on premier trains with bundled meals.

| Train Type / Quota                                | Under 5 Years (No Berth) | Ages 5–11 (No Berth / `NOSB`) | Ages 5–11 (With Berth/Seat) | Catering & Special Rules                                                 |
| :------------------------------------------------ | :----------------------- | :---------------------------- | :-------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mail / Express (`SL`, `3A`, `3E`, `2A`, `1A`)** | Free (`₹0`)              | 50% Base Fare + Fees          | 100% Adult Fare             | Pantry meals bought separately onboard or via e-catering                 |
| **Garib Rath Express (`3A` / `CC`)**              | Free (`₹0`)              | 50% Base Fare (`3A` only)     | 100% Adult Fare             | Tight 3-tier layout; full berth strongly recommended for ages 7+         |
| **Rajdhani / Duronto / Tejas**                    | Free (`₹0`)              | 50% Base Fare (`NOSB`)        | 100% Adult Fare             | Full-berth child tickets add catering fee; select **No Food** to save    |
| **Vande Bharat / Shatabdi (`CC` / `EC`)**         | Free (`₹0`)              | Not Permitted (Seated class)  | 100% Adult Fare             | Every 5–11 child needs a seat; opt out of catering if carrying home food |
| **Tatkal Quota (10 AM AC / 11 AM SL)**            | Free (`₹0`)              | Restricted / Risky in rush    | 100% Adult + Tatkal Fee     | No fare concessions in Tatkal; max 4 passengers per Tatkal PNR           |

If you book a Tatkal ticket at 10:00 AM (AC) or 11:00 AM (Sleeper), keep your family under the **4-passenger-per-PNR Tatkal limit**. Also remember that IRCTC enforces a daily **night maintenance window from 11:45 PM to 12:20 AM IST**, during which no child or adult bookings can be processed.

## How Do You Book a Child or Infant Ticket on IRCTC Website and App?

**To book a child ticket on IRCTC, enter passenger details on the booking screen: use "Add Infant without Berth" for kids under 5, and use "Add Passenger" with the "Book Berth" checkbox unticked for a 5–11 half ticket or ticked for a full berth.**

Setting up your **IRCTC Master List** ahead of time makes family bookings much faster, especially during Tatkal or peak holiday openings at 8:00 AM IST.

1. **Log in and Search Your Route:** Sign in to the IRCTC NGET website or Rail Connect mobile app. Enter your origin, destination, travel date, and preferred class (`SL`, `3E`, `3A`, `2A`, `CC`).
2. **Add Children Aged 5 to 11:** Click **+ Add Passenger**. Enter the child's exact name (matching their ID), age on journey date, and gender.
3. **Select or Unselect the Berth Option:** Look at the **Book Berth** (or _Child Berth Choice_) checkbox right next to the age box. Leave it **ticked** if you want a separate berth at full adult fare, or **untick** it to book a 50% half-fare `NOSB` ticket without a berth.
4. **Add Infants Under 5:** Scroll just below the passenger list and click **+ Add Infant without Berth**. Enter the toddler's name, age (0 to 4), and gender. Their fare remains ₹0.
5. **Choose Family Coach Preferences:** Select **Consider for Auto Upgradation** and use **Book only if confirm berths are allotted** if traveling with young children so you don't end up split across coaches.

## What Age Proof Documents Must You Carry for Children on Trains?

**You should always carry valid age proof for children aged 0 to 11, such as an Aadhaar card, birth certificate, school ID card, or passport. While children under 5 rarely face strict ID checks, TTEs routinely ask for age verification when a child looks close to age 5 or 12.**

Unlike adult passengers who must carry an original government photo ID, young children often do not have a PAN or voter ID. Indian Railways accepts both physical documents and official digital versions stored in **DigiLocker** or **mAadhaar**.

Accepted documents for child age verification include:

- **Aadhaar Card** (physical laminated card or official mAadhaar/DigiLocker PDF)
- **Municipal Birth Certificate** (best proof for toddlers aged 4–5 and kids turning 11–12)
- **School Identity Card** showing date of birth and academic year
- **Passport** (mandatory if traveling on international cross-border trains)

If a child appears older than their declared age (for example, a tall 11-year-old traveling on a half ticket or a 4-year-old traveling free) and you have no age proof, the TTE can treat the child as an adult or an under-ticketed passenger and collect the fare difference plus penalties.

## What Happens If a Child Ticket Remains on the Waiting List or Is Misdeclared?

**If a child ticket stays on the Waiting List (`WL`) after chart preparation, IRCTC automatically cancels online e-tickets and refunds the fare; children cannot board reserved coaches on unconfirmed e-tickets. Misdeclaring a child's age or boarding without a valid ticket triggers a flat ₹500 Section 138 penalty plus fare difference.**

In Indian Railways, **WL full form is Waiting List**, and every waitlisted booking follows the progression **WL (Waiting List) → RAC (Reservation Against Cancellation) → CNF (Confirmed Berth)**. Note that a half-fare `NOSB` child ticket linked to an adult PNR follows the primary adult booking status, while a full-berth child ticket occupies its own berth slot in the queue.

Under **Section 138 of the Railways Act (amended by the Jan Vishwas Act 2026)**, boarding a reserved Sleeper or AC coach with an auto-cancelled waitlisted e-ticket or an unbooked child aged 5+ without a valid ticket incurs a **flat ₹500 fine plus the single fare difference** from the originating/checking station. Only physical PRS railway counter tickets remain valid for General (`GS`) compartment travel if waitlisted.

If your family booking is stuck on a high waitlist (`WL 20+`) or shows `REGRET`, use these four legal strategies to secure confirmed seats:

1. **Split-Seat Booking via LastBerth:** Even when direct trains show Waitlist, contiguous legs on the same train often hold confirmed seats (e.g., Delhi to Kanpur in B2, then Kanpur to Patna in B4). Use [Finding Smart Seats](/) to uncover confirmed same-train combinations for your family.
2. **Current Availability (`CURR_AVBL`) Post-Charting:** Indian Railways finalizes the **First Chart ~8 to 10 hours before departure** (and the previous evening around 20:00–21:00 IST for morning trains before 14:00). Unallocated quotas are released as `CURR_AVBL` at a **10% base fare discount**, bookable up to 30 minutes before departure.
3. **Live Coach Map on Chart Vacancy:** Check empty berths coach-by-coach immediately after charting on the [Chart Vacancy Map](/chart-vacancy) so your family can book adjacent vacant seats directly.
4. **Origin-Station Quota Trick:** If intermediate station quotas (`RLWL`/`PQWL`) are full, check `GNWL` availability from the train's origin and modify your boarding point online up to 4 hours before departure.

## Common Booking Questions (FAQ)

### What is the half ticket age in Indian Railways?

The half ticket age in Indian Railways is **5 years to 11 years** (specifically 5 years completed and strictly under 12 years on the journey date). Within this bracket, you pay 50% of the adult base fare if you opt out of a separate berth (`NOSB`), or 100% adult fare if you want a dedicated berth.

### Is a train ticket free for a 4-year-old child?

Yes, a 4-year-old child travels completely free on Indian Railways as long as they share a berth or seat with an accompanying adult. You must still register the child under the **"Add Infant without Berth"** section on IRCTC so their name appears on the reservation chart for ₹0.

### Do I need a ticket for a 5-year-old child in a train?

Yes, once a child turns 5 years old on the date of travel, a paid ticket is mandatory in reserved classes. You can buy a 50% half-fare berthless ticket (`NOSB`) so the child shares your berth, or pay the full adult fare to get an individual berth or Chair Car seat.

### Will my child get a separate berth on a half ticket?

No. Since the 2016 Railway Board rule revision, a 50% half-fare child ticket (`NOSB`) does not allocate a separate seat or berth. The child must share the berth of the parent or accompanying adult; to get an independent berth, you must select "Book Berth" and pay the full adult fare.

### Can I book a child half ticket in Tatkal quota?

Under Indian Railways rules, children aged 5 to 11 traveling with a separate berth under Tatkal pay the full adult fare plus the full Tatkal surcharge. In seated Chair Cars and high-speed Tatkal windows, booking a full seat is strongly recommended because `NOSB` options are restricted in certain classes and count against the 4-passenger Tatkal PNR cap.

### How is child age calculated if a birthday falls before the travel date?

IRCTC calculates passenger age strictly as of the **date of train departure**, not the ticket booking date. If a child is 4 when you book 60 days ahead but turns 5 before travel day, you must purchase a 5-year-old child ticket; similarly, a child turning 12 before travel day needs a full adult ticket.

### Do I have to pay catering charges for a child in Rajdhani or Vande Bharat?

If you book a separate seat or berth at full fare for a child on Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Duronto, or Vande Bharat, default IRCTC catering charges are included unless you select **"No Food"** during booking. Infants under 5 traveling free without a seat do not incur mandatory meal charges.

### What age proof is accepted for children by the TTE?

Indian Railways accepts a municipal **Birth Certificate**, **Aadhaar Card**, **School ID card**, or **Passport** as valid age proof for children. Digital copies retrieved from official apps like DigiLocker or mAadhaar are also accepted by onboard TTEs during inspection.

### Can a child aged 5 to 11 travel alone on a half-fare train ticket?

No, a child aged 5 to 11 cannot travel alone on a half-fare (`NOSB`) ticket because they do not have an assigned berth and must share space with an accompanying adult passenger on the same booking. Unaccompanied minors are generally discouraged from solo long-distance rail travel without guardian supervision.

### What is NOSB on an IRCTC train ticket?

`NOSB` stands for **No Seat/Berth** on Indian Railways reservation slips and PNRs. It appears next to the name of an infant under 5 (travelling free) or a child aged 5 to 11 booked on a 50% half-fare ticket, confirming that the child is legally listed on the manifest without an individual berth.
