## Can You Get a 100% Refund If a Train Is Delayed by More Than 3 Hours?

**Yes, Indian Railways rules grant a 100% full refund of your entire ticket fare with zero clerkage or cancellation fee deduction if your train is delayed by more than three hours at your journey boarding station and you choose not to travel.**

Under standard railway cancellation rules, cancelling a confirmed ticket within 8 to 12 hours of departure results in a 50% penalty, while cancelling after chart preparation gives zero refund. However, when the railway itself causes the delay by over 3 hours (180 minutes), this penalty structure is completely waived. 

Indian Railways treats the journey as cancelled due to operational failure. You receive your base fare, reservation fee, superfast surcharge, and GST back into your original payment method. 

Here is how the 3+ hour delay refund compares to regular cancellation slabs:

| Ticket Type / Scenario | Standard Cancellation Fee | 3+ Hours Delay Refund (Passenger Not Travelled) | Mandatory Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Confirmed AC (1A/2A/3A/3E)** | ₹120 to ₹240 flat or up to 50% fare | **100% Full Refund (₹0 deduction)** | File TDR before actual train departure |
| **Confirmed Sleeper (SL)** | ₹60 to ₹120 or 50% fare | **100% Full Refund (₹0 deduction)** | File TDR before actual train departure |
| **RAC / Partially Confirmed** | ₹60 clerkage fee | **100% Full Refund (₹0 deduction)** | File TDR before actual train departure |
| **Tatkal / Premium Tatkal** | 0% refund (100% fare forfeited) | **100% Full Refund (₹0 deduction)** | File TDR before actual train departure |
| **Counter (PRS) Paper Ticket** | Standard slab deduction | **100% Full Refund (₹0 deduction)** | Surrender ticket at PRS counter before departure |

## How Do You File an IRCTC TDR for a Delayed Train? (Step-by-Step)

**To file an IRCTC TDR for a delayed train, log into irctc.co.in or the RailConnect app, go to Booked Ticket History, select your PNR, click File TDR, choose the reason "Train Late by More Than 3 Hours and Passenger Not Travelled", and submit before departure.**

The process is 100% digital for online e-tickets. Follow these exact steps to make sure your refund is processed smoothly without zonal railway rejections:

1. **Log in to IRCTC:** Open the official IRCTC website (`irctc.co.in`) or the IRCTC RailConnect mobile app.
2. **Open Booked Ticket History:** Navigate to **My Account** $\rightarrow$ **My Transactions** $\rightarrow$ **Booked Ticket History**.
3. **Select Your Delayed PNR:** Click on the active booking for the delayed train.
4. **Initiate TDR:** Scroll down and click the **File TDR** button.
5. **Select Passengers:** Check the boxes next to the passengers who are not travelling. (If some passengers travel while others do not, select only those cancelling).
6. **Choose the Exact TDR Reason:** From the drop-down menu, select **"Train Late by More Than 3 Hours and Passenger Not Travelled"**.
7. **Submit and Save Acknowledgment:** Confirm your submission. IRCTC generates a unique TDR Reference Number.

Once filed, IRCTC sends the claim to the concerned Zonal Railway's Chief Commercial Manager (Refunds). Because train delay timestamps are verified automatically against the National Train Enquiry System (NTES), valid delay claims are approved and credited to your original bank account or card within 5 to 7 business days.

## When Is the Strict Deadline to File a TDR for a Delayed Train?

**The strict deadline to file an IRCTC TDR for a train delayed by more than three hours is before the actual departure of the train from your scheduled boarding station. If you file after the train departs, your refund claim will be permanently rejected.**

This is the single biggest mistake passengers make during major railway delays. Many travellers wait until the next morning or until the train reaches its destination to file their claim. 

Railway rules explicitly measure the deadline against the **actual departure time**, not the original scheduled timetable departure time:

- **Example:** If your train was scheduled to depart at 4:00 PM but is delayed until 8:30 PM (4.5 hours late), you can file your TDR anytime up until 8:29 PM. 
- **The Cutoff:** The moment the train physically leaves your boarding platform at 8:30 PM, the IRCTC system locks out the TDR reason. Any subsequent filing will be rejected because the Travelling Ticket Examiner (TTE) prepares the chart and marks berths on their Handheld Terminal (HHT).

If you are unsure whether your train will make up time along the route, check live running status on NTES or track your charting status on [Chart Times](/chart-times).

## Are You Entitled to Free Meals When Your Train Is Delayed?

**Yes, passengers holding confirmed or RAC tickets on premium trains with bundled catering (Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Duronto, Vande Bharat, Tejas) and mail/express trains with attached pantry cars are entitled to complimentary meals and drinking water if the train is delayed by more than 2 hours during scheduled meal times.**

Under the Indian Railways Passenger Citizen Charter and IRCTC Catering Policy, train delays caused by operational snags, fog, track maintenance, or weather trigger mandatory free catering. 

Key conditions for free meal entitlements include:
- **Eligible Trains:** All Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Duronto, Vande Bharat, Tejas Express, and Mail/Express trains with operational pantry cars.
- **Minimum Delay Threshold:** The train must be running late by more than **2 hours** (for premium trains) or **3 hours** (for regular express trains).
- **Meal Time Window:** The delay must overlap with regular meal hours (Breakfast: 7:00 AM – 9:00 AM, Lunch: 12:30 PM – 2:00 PM, Evening Tea: 4:30 PM – 6:00 PM, Dinner: 7:30 PM – 9:30 PM).
- **Zero Cost:** Attendants and pantry staff cannot charge any money for these delay meals. A mandatory 1-litre bottle of Rail Neer packaged drinking water is also provided free.

**IRCTC serves standardized complimentary meal packs during train delays depending on the time of day: tea and biscuits for morning delays, vegetarian breakfast packs with bread and butter, and rice, dal, and pickle combos for lunch and dinner delays.**

The catering contractor on board is mandated by the Railway Board to serve the following complimentary items based on the delay duration:

If onboard pantry staff refuse to provide complimentary meals during an eligible delay, dial **RailMadad on 139** or register an immediate complaint on the RailMadad mobile app with your PNR and coach number.

## How Do You Claim a Full Refund for a Physical Counter Ticket?

**To claim a full refund for a physical counter ticket when a train is delayed by over 3 hours, surrender the original paper ticket at the station PRS ticket counter before the actual departure of the train, or collect a TDR certificate from the Station Master.**

For tickets purchased at railway station counters, the procedure differs from online e-tickets:

1. **Surrender at PRS Counter (Recommended):** Take your original paper ticket to any computerized Passenger Reservation System (PRS) counter at the station before the train's actual departure. The booking clerk cancels the ticket immediately and pays 100% of the cash fare across the counter with zero clerkage fee.
2. **If PRS Counters Are Closed (Station Master TDR):** If the counter is closed at night or the station has no active PRS window, meet the **Station Master (SM) or Station Superintendent**.
3. **Obtain an EFT / TDR Certificate:** The Station Master will issue an Excess Fare Ticket (EFT) or a physical TDR receipt certifying that the train was delayed by >3 hours and that the passenger did not board.
4. **Submit for Refund Within 72 Hours:** Present this physical TDR certificate and original ticket to the Chief Commercial Manager (Refunds) of the concerned zonal railway within **72 hours** to collect your refund.

## What If You Miss a Connecting Train Due to a Late Train?

**If your first train is delayed and causes you to miss a connecting train on a linked PNR, you are entitled to a 100% full refund on the second ticket with zero cancellation fees, provided you file a TDR within 3 hours of the first train's actual arrival.**

Indian Railways offers the **Connecting Journey Booking (Link PNR)** feature to protect passengers against missed connections:

- **Linked PNR Protection:** When booking on IRCTC, selecting "Connecting Journey" links both tickets under a single journey chain.
- **Refund Policy:** If Train 1 arrives late and the layover time between arrival and the second train's departure becomes impossible to meet, the second ticket is fully refunded without cancellation fees.
- **Filing Deadline:** You must file the online TDR or surrender the counter ticket within **3 hours of the actual arrival of the first train** at the interchange station.
- **TDR Reason to Select:** Choose **"Missed Connecting Train due to Late Running of Main Train"**.

For separate, unlinked PNRs, standard cancellation rules apply unless the Station Master issues a manual delay certificate at the connecting junction.

## What Happens If You Decide to Travel on the Delayed Train?

**If you decide to board and travel on the delayed train, you cannot claim a fare refund. The TTE will verify your ticket and mark you present on their Handheld Terminal (HHT), which automatically invalidates any filed TDR.**

Many passengers attempt to file a TDR online to claim a refund while simultaneously boarding the coach. This will not work:

- **HHT Live Attendance Sync:** TTEs use digital Handheld Terminals connected to central PRS servers. When the TTE scans your QR code or checks your berth, your status is updated to **"Turned Up / Travelled"**.
- **TDR Rejection:** During post-journey reconciliation, the Chief Commercial Manager checks the HHT attendance log. If your name appears as travelled, your TDR claim is rejected for fraud.
- **Berth Vacancy Tracking:** If you want to check which berths opened up because other passengers cancelled due to the delay, use [Coach Journey Lookup](/seat-status) or inspect available seats on [Chart Vacancy](/chart-vacancy).

## Common Booking Questions (FAQ)

### Can I get a full refund if my train is 2 hours and 50 minutes late?
No, the 100% full refund rule requires a minimum delay of more than 3 hours (at least 3 hours and 1 minute) at your boarding station. For delays under 3 hours, standard IRCTC cancellation slabs apply.

### Do I get a full refund on Tatkal tickets if the train is delayed by over 3 hours?
Yes, Tatkal and Premium Tatkal tickets are eligible for a 100% full refund with zero cancellation charges if the train is delayed by more than 3 hours at your boarding station and you file a TDR before actual departure.

### What happens if the train is delayed by 3 hours at the destination but departed on time?
You cannot claim a full ticket fare refund if the train departed on time and you completed the journey. Delay refunds apply only when you choose not to travel due to a >3 hour delay at your boarding station.

### How long does an IRCTC delay TDR refund take to credit?
Online TDR refunds for trains delayed by more than 3 hours typically take between 5 to 7 business days to process and credit back to your original bank account, card, or UPI wallet.

### Can RAC passengers claim a full refund if the train is running late?
Yes, RAC and waitlisted passengers on e-tickets or counter tickets can claim a 100% refund with zero clerkage fee if the train is delayed by more than 3 hours and a TDR is filed before departure.

### Can I get free food on a normal Mail or Express train during delays?
Yes, if the Mail or Express train has an attached pantry car and is delayed by more than 3 hours overlapping with meal times, IRCTC catering is required to provide complimentary meals.

### What if the IRCTC website is down during the train delay?
If the IRCTC website is down during late hours, call the railway helpline at 139 or visit the nearest PRS counter or Station Master office before actual train departure to log your refund claim.

### Do I need a TTE certificate to file a delay TDR for an e-ticket?
No, for online e-tickets, you do not need a physical TTE certificate because IRCTC automatically cross-references the official train delay timestamps recorded in the National Train Enquiry System (NTES).
