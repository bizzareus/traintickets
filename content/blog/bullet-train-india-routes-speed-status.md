## TL;DR
India's first Bullet Train project is a high-speed rail corridor connecting Mumbai and Ahmedabad over a distance of 508 km. Utilizing Japanese Shinkansen technology, the train will run at an operational speed of 320 km/h, reducing travel time to 2 hours. Trial runs are planned for 2026, with full operations targeted by 2028.

---

## What is the Bullet Train Project in India?

**The bullet train project in India is the country’s first high-speed rail corridor, connecting Mumbai and Ahmedabad. Spanning 508 kilometers, the project uses Japanese Shinkansen E5 technology, promising to revolutionize interstate transit by drastically reducing travel times between the two financial hubs.**

Officially known as the **Mumbai-Ahmedabad High-Speed Rail (MAHSR)** project, the corridor is being executed by the National High-Speed Rail Corporation Limited (NHSRCL). It represents a technological leap for Indian infrastructure, bringing Japanese bullet train (Shinkansen) standards to India.

The project is designed to handle high passenger volumes safely and efficiently. Once completed, it will serve as the benchmark for several other proposed high-speed corridors across India, including Delhi-Varanasi, Delhi-Ahmedabad, and Mumbai-Nagpur.

---

## What is the Speed of the Bullet Train in India?

**The bullet train in India will operate at a design speed of 350 km/h and an operational speed of 320 km/h. This high speed will cut the Mumbai to Ahmedabad travel time from over 6 hours by superfast train to just 2 hours.**

To achieve these speeds, the train runs on a dedicated standard-gauge track built on an elevated viaduct. This ensures zero interference from road traffic, cattle, or local rail networks.

Compared to current premium trains like the Vande Bharat (which operates at a maximum speed of 130–160 km/h on mixed tracks), the bullet train will run twice as fast. It will feature advanced safety systems like automatic train control and seismic detection systems to stop the train during earthquakes.

## What is the Route and Station List for the Bullet Train?

**The route for the Mumbai-Ahmedabad bullet train features 12 stations: Mumbai (BKC), Thane, Virar, Boisar, Vapi, Bilimora, Surat, Bharuch, Vadodara, Anand, Ahmedabad, and Sabarmati. Most of the track is elevated, with a 21 km tunnel including an undersea segment in Maharashtra.**

The 508-kilometer corridor spans across the states of Maharashtra and Gujarat, along with the Union Territory of Dadra and Nagar Haveli.

### Bullet Train Route Stations

*   **Maharashtra Stations:** Mumbai (Bandra Kurla Complex), Thane, Virar, and Boisar.
*   **Gujarat Stations:** Vapi, Bilimora, Surat, Bharuch, Vadodara, Anand, Ahmedabad, and Sabarmati.

Out of the total distance, 348 km lies in Gujarat, 156 km in Maharashtra, and 4 km in Dadra and Nagar Haveli. The Mumbai terminal at Bandra Kurla Complex (BKC) is built underground to avoid urban congestion. The route also includes a 21-kilometer underground tunnel between BKC and Kalyan Shilphata, featuring a 7-kilometer stretch built under the sea (Thane Creek).

## When Will the Bullet Train in India Start Operating?

**The bullet train in India is projected to start trial runs on the Surat-Bilimora section by 2026, with full operations on the Mumbai-Ahmedabad corridor expected by 2028. Progress continues with viaduct construction and station building across Gujarat and Maharashtra.**

Work on the MAHSR corridor has accelerated rapidly, with Gujarat leading the infrastructure progress:

1.  **Surat-Bilimora Trial Runs (2026):** Trial runs on this 48 km section in Gujarat are expected to begin by 2026 using Japanese Shinkansen E5 train sets.
2.  **Full Corridor Operations (2028):** The complete Mumbai-Ahmedabad stretch is targeted for commercial launch by 2028, pending tunnel works and land acquisition clearances in Maharashtra.
3.  **Construction Updates:** Over 300 kilometers of the elevated viaduct structure have already been completed, and station foundations are being laid across all major hubs in Gujarat.
